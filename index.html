Understanding Panchang

Imagine the sky is a giant pizza divided into 360 degrees.

Both the Sun and Moon move around this pizza. The Moon moves much faster (about 12° per day) than the Sun (about 1° per day).

A "Tithi" is the time it takes for the Moon to get exactly 12 degrees farther away from the Sun.

So, every 12° slice of difference = 1 Tithi. There are 30 Tithis in a lunar month (30 slices x 12° = 360°).

Think of the Sun and Moon as runners on a circular track.

  • Amavasya (New Moon): Both are at the starting line together (0° difference).
  • Shukla Paksha (Waxing): The Moon races ahead. When it's 180° ahead (halfway around), it's Purnima.
  • Purnima (Full Moon): The Moon is directly opposite the Sun.
  • Krishna Paksha (Waning): The Moon continues the second half of the lap to catch up to the Sun again.
